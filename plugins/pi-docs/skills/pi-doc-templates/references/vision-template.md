# Product Vision & Charter

## Purpose

One-paragraph statement of why this product exists. What change does it make in the world?

## Problem Statement

What specific problem are users experiencing today? Be concrete. Quantify the pain where possible (time wasted, money lost, friction points). Cite research or firsthand observation.

## Target Users

Define each user segment as a persona:

### [Persona Name] — [Role]

- **Who they are**: Demographics, technical skill level, context of use.
- **What they need**: The job they're trying to get done.
- **Current workaround**: How they solve this today without your product.
- **Frequency**: How often they encounter this problem.

Repeat for each persona (typically 2-4).

## Value Proposition

For [target user], who [statement of need], [product name] is a [product category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], our product [primary differentiator].

## Business Model

How the product generates revenue:

- **Pricing model**: (subscription, usage-based, freemium, one-time, etc.)
- **Target price point**: Range or specific tier structure.
- **Unit economics**: CAC, LTV, margins (estimates acceptable for early stage).

## Market Opportunity

- **Total Addressable Market (TAM)**: Size and source of estimate.
- **Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**: The realistic slice you can reach.
- **Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)**: What you expect to capture in 1-3 years.

## Success Metrics

Measurable outcomes that indicate the product is working:

- **North star metric**: The single metric that best captures user value.
- **Leading indicators**: Metrics that predict success (activation rate, engagement frequency).
- **Lagging indicators**: Metrics that confirm success (revenue, retention, NPS).
